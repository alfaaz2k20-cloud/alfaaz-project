# Alfaaz Recruit: Research Basis, Construct Rationale, and Pre-Implementation Measurement Lock

**Status:** Research-informed design and measurement rationale.  
**Current claim boundary:** This document does **not** claim that the exact Alfaaz Recruit games have been empirically validated. It documents the research basis for the behavioral domains, assessment method, task-design logic, and planned reliability/validity checks that should govern implementation.

> **Core statement:** Alfaaz Recruit is a **research-informed, theoretically grounded assessment design**, not a validated psychometric instrument. The exact construct validity, reliability, discriminant validity, and criterion validity of the Alfaaz game battery remain empirical questions.

---

## 1. Purpose

Alfaaz Recruit combines:

1. a seven-scenario Situational Judgment Test (SJT); and
2. seven game worlds containing three behavioral mini-games each.

The game battery is intended to provide **observed behavioral evidence** that can be examined alongside the SJT and, in a later empirical study, against established measures and relevant real-world outcomes.

The exact Alfaaz games are original task designs. Therefore:

- research on a construct is **not** validation of an Alfaaz game;
- research on game-based assessment is **not** validation of the Alfaaz battery;
- a plausible behavioral indicator is **not** automatically a psychological measure;
- a passing software test is **not** evidence of construct validity.

The project should therefore distinguish four layers:

```text
Research basis
      ↓
Design rationale
      ↓
Correct implementation
      ↓
Empirical validation
```

Each layer answers a different question.

# 2. General Assessment Method

## 2.1 Situational Judgment Tests

SJT research provides a substantial evidence base for using job-relevant situations and response judgments to obtain evidence about a variety of constructs. Research also shows that the constructs assessed depend on the design and response instructions, so Alfaaz must keep the intended construct interpretation explicit.

Relevant research:

- Christian, M. S., Edwards, B. D., & Bradley, J. C. (2010). *Situational Judgment Tests: Constructs Assessed and a Meta-Analysis of Their Criterion-Related Validities.* Personnel Psychology, 63, 83–117. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1744-6570.2009.01163.x
- McDaniel, M. A., Hartman, N. S., Whetzel, D. L., & Grubb, W. L. III. (2007). *Situational Judgment Tests, Response Instructions, and Validity: A Meta-Analysis.* Personnel Psychology, 60, 63–91. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1744-6570.2007.00065.x

Christian et al. emphasize that SJTs can be designed to assess different construct domains and report stronger criterion-related validity when the predictor construct is matched appropriately to the criterion facet. That supports retaining an explicit construct map for the Alfaaz SJT rather than treating the SJT as construct-free.

## 2.2 Game-Based Assessment

Game-related assessment is a legitimate research area, but the literature strongly cautions that **game design can change what is being measured**.

Relevant research:

- Ramos-Villagrasa, P. J., Fernández-del-Río, E., & Castro, Á. (2022). *Game-related assessments for personnel selection: A systematic review.* Frontiers in Psychology, 13, 952002. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.952002
- Landers, R. N., & Sanchez, D. R. (2022). *Game-based, gamified, and gamefully designed assessments for employee selection: Definitions, distinctions, design, and validation.* International Journal of Selection and Assessment. https://doi.org/10.1111/ijsa.12376
- Wiernik, B. M., et al. (2022). *Developing and validating a serious game-based assessment for cyber occupations in the US Air Force.* International Journal of Selection and Assessment. https://doi.org/10.1111/ijsa.12378

The systematic-review literature is especially important for Alfaaz: findings on construct validity of game-related assessments are mixed, and the type and design of the game can materially affect validity. Game-related assessments therefore need ordinary psychometric scrutiny, including reliability, construct validity, criterion validity, and bias/fairness analysis.

**Design rule for Alfaaz:** Use game mechanics to elicit observable behavior. Do not treat a mechanic such as clicking, speed, exploration, or persistence as self-validating evidence of a psychological trait.

# 3. Validity Framework

The Alfaaz design follows the validity framework used in modern psychological and educational testing.

The *Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing* emphasize that validity concerns the evidence supporting the interpretation and use of test scores for a stated purpose, with multiple sources of evidence rather than a single coefficient.

Relevant guidance:

- American Educational Research Association, American Psychological Association, & National Council on Measurement in Education. (2014). *Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing.*

https://www.aera.net/Publications/Books/Standards-for-Educational-Psychological-Testing-2014-Edition

For Alfaaz, the practical validity argument is:

```text
CONTENT
Does the task represent the intended behavioral facet?

        +

RESPONSE PROCESS
Does the observed behavior arise from the intended task demands?

        +

INTERNAL STRUCTURE
Do related observations behave coherently?

        +

RELATIONS TO OTHER VARIABLES
Does the game evidence relate as expected to the SJT and other relevant measures?

        +

CRITERION RELATION
Does the evidence relate to relevant real-world volunteer outcomes?

        ↓

VALIDITY ARGUMENT
```

The current project only establishes the **design-side rationale**. It does not yet establish the later empirical components.

# 4. Construct-by-Construct Research Rationale

## 4.1 Empathy — The Frequency

**Alfaaz target:** Sensitivity to another person's cues and context-appropriate adjustment.

Perspective-taking is closely related to empathy and can be differentiated into cognitive and affective components. Research also indicates that executive processes can contribute to perspective-taking, which is important because the task must not accidentally become a generic executive-function test.

Relevant research:

- Healey, M. L., & Grossman, M. (2018). *Cognitive and Affective Perspective-Taking: Evidence for Shared and Dissociable Anatomical Substrates.* Frontiers in Neurology, 9, 491. https://doi.org/10.3389/fneur.2018.00491

**Design implication**

The Frequency should provide repeated opportunities to:

- detect a partner-state cue;
- update behavior after the cue;
- distinguish accommodation from clarification or task pursuit when context changes;
- avoid a universal rule such as "accommodate = good."

The task should include counterbalanced/contextualized trials where different responses are appropriate.

**Nuisance demands**

- reading/language;
- cue salience;
- compliance;
- task comprehension;
- mental-set switching.

**What the game cannot establish**

The Frequency cannot by itself establish a global empathy trait or an internal emotional capacity. It provides behavioral evidence about a narrower social-responsiveness facet.

---

## 4.2 Conscientiousness — The Archive

**Alfaaz target:** Careful, dependable task behavior involving accuracy, verification, consistency, and follow-through.

There is extensive occupational research on conscientiousness. A large quantitative synthesis links conscientiousness with goal-directed performance, interpersonal responsibility, commitment, perseverance, self-regulation, and proficient performance. Research on narrow traits also supports the usefulness of examining specific facets rather than assuming every task captures the entire broad trait.

Relevant research:

- Wilmot, M. P., & Ones, D. S. (2019). *A century of research on conscientiousness at work.* Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 116, 23004–23010. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1908430116
- Dudley, N. M., Orvis, K. A., Lebiecki, J. E., & Cortina, J. M. (2006). *A meta-analytic investigation of conscientiousness in the prediction of job performance: Examining the intercorrelations and the incremental validity of narrow traits.* Journal of Applied Psychology, 91, 40–57. https://doi.org/10.1037/0021-9010.91.1.40

**Design implication**

The Archive should distinguish:

- accurate work;
- verification under uncertainty;
- exception handling;
- self-correction;
- quality control.

"More guide openings = more conscientiousness" is explicitly invalid as a design assumption.

**Nuisance demands**

- working memory;
- reading;
- prior familiarity;
- rule complexity.

**Control**

Rules should remain available so that the construct is not simply memory capacity.

---

## 4.3 Collaborative Spirit — The Shared Canvas

**Alfaaz target:** Coordination, need-sensitive cooperation, role adjustment, and repair of collaborative misalignment.

Teamwork research emphasizes coordinated processes and multiple dimensions of teamwork rather than a single altruistic behavior. A systematic review and meta-analysis of controlled teamwork interventions found effects across multiple teamwork dimensions and contexts, supporting the importance of treating teamwork as a multidimensional behavioral domain.

Relevant research:

- McEwan, D., Ruissen, G. R., Eys, M. A., Zumbo, B. D., & Beauchamp, M. R. (2017). *The Effectiveness of Teamwork Training on Teamwork Behaviors and Team Performance: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Controlled Interventions.* PLOS ONE, 12(1), e0169604. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0169604
- Burke, C. S., Stagl, K. C., Salas, E., Pierce, L., & Kendall, D. (2006). *Understanding team adaptation: A conceptual analysis and model.* Journal of Applied Psychology, 91, 1189–1207. https://doi.org/10.1037/0021-9010.91.6.1189

**Design implication**

The Shared Canvas should emphasize:

- coordination;
- adjustment to partner pace;
- appropriate resource cooperation;
- repair after misalignment.

"More sharing = better" is forbidden. Include control trials where sharing is unnecessary.

**Nuisance demands**

- motor coordination;
- spatial ability;
- task familiarity;
- frustration;
- social interpretation.

---

## 4.4 Emotional Agility — The Shifting Grid

**Alfaaz target:** Behavioral adaptation and recovery when conditions change or a setback occurs.

Research on regulatory flexibility argues against assuming that one regulatory strategy is always adaptive. Adaptation depends partly on person-situation interaction and the ability to change strategies as circumstances change.

Relevant research:

- Bonanno, G. A., & Burton, C. L. (2013). *Regulatory Flexibility: An Individual Differences Perspective on Coping and Emotion Regulation.* Perspectives on Psychological Science, 8, 591–612. https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691613504116

**Design implication**

The Shifting Grid should generate behavioral opportunities for:

- recovery after a scripted interruption;
- adaptation after a rule or requirement change;
- reduction in perseveration;
- strategy change;
- re-establishment of task rhythm.

**Critical interpretation limit**

This is **behavioral flexibility/adaptation evidence**, not direct measurement of an internal emotional state.

**Nuisance demands**

- cognitive flexibility;
- processing speed;
- task-switching;
- frustration tolerance;
- interface response demands.

No loud sounds, distress imagery, false error claims, or harmful stimulation.

---

## 4.5 Curiosity — The Hidden Gallery

**Alfaaz target:** Voluntary information seeking and exploration under uncertainty, including exploration without immediate instrumental reward.

Curiosity research links uncertainty and information seeking, and emphasizes that information-seeking can itself be an important behavioral expression of curiosity.

Relevant research:

- Gottlieb, J., Oudeyer, P.-Y., Lopes, M., & Baranes, A. (2013). *Information-seeking, curiosity, and attention: Computational and neural mechanisms.* Trends in Cognitive Sciences, 17, 585–593. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2013.09.001
- Kidd, C., & Hayden, B. Y. (2015). *The Psychology and Neuroscience of Curiosity.* Neuron, 88, 449–460. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2015.10.004

**Design implication**

The Hidden Gallery should provide:

- optional discovery;
- investigation of unexplained information;
- breadth and depth of exploration;
- later use of previously discovered information.

More clicks must not automatically equal more curiosity.

**Nuisance demands**

- domain interest;
- reading;
- planning;
- risk preference;
- familiarity with exploration games.

---

## 4.6 Creative Initiative — The Broken Tool

**Alfaaz target:** Generating and testing alternative approaches when standard procedures are insufficient.

Creativity research treats creative cognition as heterogeneous and involving interactions among memory, attention, cognitive control, divergent processes, and convergent processes.

Relevant research:

- Benedek, M., & Fink, A. (2019). *Toward a cognitive neuroscience of creative cognition: A review.* Current Opinion in Behavioral Sciences, 27, 116–122. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cobeha.2018.08.005
- Zhang, W., Sjoerds, Z., & Hommel, B. (2020). *Metacontrol of human creativity: The neurocognitive mechanisms of convergent and divergent thinking.* NeuroImage, 210, 116572. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2019.116572

**Design implication**

The Broken Tool should provide:

- multiple valid solutions;
- opportunities to generate and test hypotheses;
- feedback that supports strategy revision;
- logging of attempt → outcome → feedback response → next attempt.

Clicks, number of objects touched, or unusual movement alone are not creativity measures.

**Nuisance demands**

- fluid reasoning;
- spatial reasoning;
- prior puzzle experience;
- task familiarity;
- working memory.

First-try success is not penalized. Lack of an opportunity to experiment means insufficient evidence.

---

## 4.7 Motivation — The Repetition

**Alfaaz target:** Voluntary persistence after a clearly stated minimum, with continuation remaining optional.

Persistence is a major topic in motivation and self-regulation research, but contemporary models also treat appropriate disengagement as important. This directly supports Alfaaz's decision not to treat "more persistence is always better."

Relevant research:

- Brandstätter, V., & Bernecker, K. (2022). *Persistence and Disengagement in Personal Goal Pursuit.* Annual Review of Psychology, 73, 271–299. https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-020821-110710

Volunteer motivation is also multidimensional, with different motives and forms of regulation contributing to volunteering. Therefore, persistence in a routine assessment task must not be interpreted as a complete measure of volunteer motivation.

**Design implication**

The Repetition should include:

- an explicitly stated minimum;
- explicit permission to stop;
- optional continuation;
- reduced immediate feedback/reward in at least one condition;
- a ceiling that is treated as right-censored evidence rather than as "the longer the better" motivation.

Stopping after the stated minimum is neutral.

**Nuisance demands**

- boredom tolerance;
- compliance;
- fatigue;
- task interest;
- impression management;
- personal time constraints.

# 5. Pre-Implementation Research & Measurement Lock

This section is a **design gate**. It exists specifically to prevent the project from hard-wiring unexamined measurement assumptions into code.

## 5.1 Rule

Before implementing or repairing any missing/partial behavioral extractor, every mini-game must have a research-and-measurement specification that answers:

```text
parameter
    ↓
behavioral facet
    ↓
nearest established construct
    ↓
research basis
    ↓
mechanism expected to elicit behavior
    ↓
observable indicators
    ↓
nuisance demands
    ↓
control/decoy conditions
    ↓
expected SJT relationship
    ↓
expected sibling-game relationship
    ↓
implementation specification
```

No production feature should exist merely because it is technically easy to calculate.

## 5.2 Required Design Sheet fields

Every mini-game Design Sheet should explicitly contain:

1. Target parameter.
2. Behavioral facet.
3. Nearest researched construct, if one exists.
4. Research citations.
5. Mechanistic rationale linking task behavior to construct.
6. Trial structure and stimulus variation.
7. Raw telemetry events.
8. Feature formula, units, inputs, and intended direction.
9. Nuisance demands and likely alternative explanations.
10. Control/decoy conditions.
11. Minimum observations required for usable evidence.
12. Ceiling behavior and censoring rules.
13. Accessibility alternatives and latency exclusions.
14. What constitutes insufficient evidence.
15. What the task **cannot** establish.
16. Expected relationship with the SJT.
17. Expected relationship with the other two mini-games.
18. Any proposal not already authorized by V2, marked `PROPOSED: needs owner approval`.

## 5.3 Design checks before code

Before an extractor is implemented, the design should pass these checks:

### Construct relevance

The task must elicit the target behavior for a reason that can be stated explicitly.

### Multiple opportunities

A parameter should not depend on one event or one lucky trial.

### Nuisance control

The same behavior should not be explainable almost entirely by speed, memory, motor precision, reading, or game familiarity unless that demand is itself relevant and explicitly documented.

### Control conditions

Where a simplistic "good behavior" can be inferred, include trials where that behavior is not contextually appropriate.

### Behavioral observability

The intended construct must be expressed as something the system can actually observe.

### Feature sensitivity

Changing relevant events should change the feature.

### Noise invariance

Changing irrelevant events should not determine the feature.

These last two are **implementation/pipeline tests**, not construct-validity tests.

### Accessibility neutrality

Any feature strongly dependent on latency or motor interaction must have an approved alternative or be excluded in the applicable accessibility mode.

### No hard-wired normative assumptions

Do not choose production thresholds simply because a value "sounds high" or "sounds low."

# 6. Reliability: What Alfaaz Will Actually Try to Establish

Reliability should **not** be defined as agreement between the SJT and the games.

Instead:

## 6.1 SJT ↔ game evidence

This is primarily evidence relevant to **convergent validity**.

For each parameter:

```text
SJT
 ↕
Game 1
 ↕
Game 2
 ↕
Game 3
```

We examine:

- SJT ↔ Game 1;
- SJT ↔ Game 2;
- SJT ↔ Game 3.

The question is:

> Do different assessment methods intended to bear on the same parameter show the expected relationship?

A meaningful relationship supports the validity argument. A weak relationship is not automatically a failure: it may indicate construct mismatch, method variance, task contamination, or that the two methods capture different facets.

## 6.2 Game ↔ game evidence

For each parameter:

```text
Game 1 ↔ Game 2
Game 1 ↔ Game 3
Game 2 ↔ Game 3
```

This provides evidence relevant to:

- internal structure;
- consistency across task contexts;
- task-specific variance;
- measurement error.

Appropriate analyses may include:

- inter-task correlations;
- intraclass correlation where assumptions are satisfied;
- Generalizability Theory to partition person, task, and residual sources of variance;
- dimensionality analysis where the number and type of observations support it.

**Do not automatically use Cronbach's alpha.** Three deliberately different behavioral tasks are not equivalent to three interchangeable questionnaire items.

## 6.3 The "least error" objective

The design objective should be:

> **Use multiple complementary observations so that task-specific noise contributes less to the overall evidence, without forcing the tasks to become redundant.**

This means:

- high convergence can be useful;
- excessive convergence may simply mean the three games are copies of one another;
- disagreement must be investigated rather than automatically averaged away;
- the best three-game design is not necessarily the design with the highest correlation between all three tasks.

A useful conceptual decomposition is:

```text
Observed behavior
=
common construct-related variance
+
task-specific variance
+
nuisance variance
+
random error
```

The design should attempt to increase the first component and reduce the latter three, especially nuisance variance.

# 7. Reliability/Validity Analysis Plan for Later Human Data

When empirical data become available, the analysis should proceed separately for each parameter.

## Step 1: Data quality

Check:

- missingness;
- invalidity;
- interruption;
- accessibility mode;
- device/input modality;
- event sequence integrity;
- feature distributions;
- ceiling effects;
- floor effects.

## Step 2: Feature-level behavior

Determine whether the intended events actually produce interpretable feature distributions.

Reject or revise features that are:

- effectively constant;
- dominated by technical failure;
- dominated by accessibility/device effects;
- extremely sparse;
- unrelated to the behavior they were designed to represent.

## Step 3: Within-parameter structure

For the three mini-games:

```text
A1, A2, A3
```

or the equivalent for each parameter, examine whether they show enough common variance to justify a shared parameter-level interpretation.

## Step 4: SJT convergence

Compare the game evidence separately with the SJT evidence.

Do not average them first.

## Step 5: Discriminant evidence

Where feasible, examine theoretically unrelated constructs or nuisance measures.

Example:

A creativity-oriented task that correlates almost entirely with processing speed or general reasoning may be measuring something other than intended.

## Step 6: Measurement error

Estimate how much evidence varies because of:

- person;
- task;
- occasion;
- residual error;
- accessibility/device effects.

## Step 7: Refinement

Only after examining the empirical evidence should Alfaaz consider:

- removing a weak mini-game;
- revising a feature;
- changing trial composition;
- reducing nuisance demands;
- revising a construct interpretation.

Any such revision becomes a new versioned design and scoring version.

# 8. Why the Three Mini-Games Are Designed to Differ

The three mini-games per parameter are **not three copies for the purpose of inflating reliability**.

They are deliberately different in mechanics and nuisance demands.

The design hypothesis is:

```text
same behavioral target
       +
different task contexts
       ↓
less dependence on one narrow mechanic
       ↓
potentially stronger evidence
```

This is a hypothesis that must later be tested.

The opposite outcome is also possible:

```text
different mechanics
       ↓
different constructs actually elicited
       ↓
weak convergence
       ↓
task-specific contamination
```

That result would be useful evidence and should lead to redesign rather than forced aggregation.

# 9. Research-Backed Design Rules for the 21 Games

The following rules should be treated as **pre-implementation constraints**:

### The Frequency

Do not define empathy as:

- time in a comfort zone;
- always accommodating;
- always prioritizing the partner.

Require context-sensitive cue response.

### The Archive

Do not define conscientiousness as:

- number of guide opens;
- maximum checking;
- speed.

Measure outcome and process together.

### The Shared Canvas

Do not define collaboration as:

- maximum sharing;
- always matching the partner's pace;
- pure motor synchronization.

Require context-sensitive cooperation and repair.

### The Shifting Grid

Do not define emotional agility as:

- raw reaction time;
- absence of all errors;
- direct emotion.

Measure behavioral recovery/adaptation.

### The Hidden Gallery

Do not define curiosity as:

- click count;
- exploration for rewards;
- reckless exploration.

Measure voluntary information seeking and information use.

### The Broken Tool

Do not define creativity as:

- unusual clicks;
- number of attempts;
- first-try failure.

Measure generation/testing of alternatives and strategy adaptation.

### The Repetition

Do not define motivation as:

- longest possible completion;
- compliance;
- inability to stop.

Measure voluntary persistence with an explicit stopping point and recognize appropriate disengagement.

# 12. What the Research Document Must Not Become

This document must never be used to justify:

- automated selection decisions;
- a personality diagnosis;
- normative applicant rankings;
- unvalidated game thresholds;
- claims that a specific game is "proven" to measure a construct;
- replacing the SJT with game evidence;
- interpreting divergence as dishonesty or character.

Its purpose is to constrain design and document the scientific rationale.

# 13. Permanent Core Statement

> **Alfaaz Recruit's game battery is research-informed, theoretically grounded, and designed according to established principles of assessment and construct measurement. The individual games are original Alfaaz task designs. The research literature supports the behavioral domains and assessment methodology but does not validate the exact Alfaaz tasks. Before implementation of behavioral scoring, each game must have an explicit construct-to-behavior rationale, nuisance analysis, control conditions, observable indicators, and expected relationships with the SJT and sibling games. Later human-data analysis will evaluate reliability, construct convergence, discriminant evidence, measurement error, accessibility effects, and criterion relationships.**
